Common Spider Plant problems

Brown leaf tips

Symptoms: Crispy brown tips on many leaves.
Likely cause: Fluoride or chlorine in tap water, low humidity, or fertilizer salt buildup.
What to do: Water with filtered or distilled water, trim the brown tips, and flush the soil periodically.

Pale, washed-out leaves

Symptoms: Leaves lose their green-and-white variegation and look faded.
Likely cause: Too much direct sun.
What to do: Move to bright but indirect light.

Spider mites

Symptoms: Fine webbing and stippled, speckled leaves.
Likely cause: Dry indoor air.
What to do: Rinse leaves, raise humidity, and treat with insecticidal soap if the infestation persists.

FAQ

Is a spider plant toxic to cats and dogs?

No, it's considered non-toxic, though eating a large amount can cause mild digestive upset, and some cats get a mild stimulant reaction to it.

Why does my spider plant have brown tips?

Most often tap-water chemicals or fertilizer salt buildup — try filtered water and occasional deep watering to flush the soil.

How do spider plant "babies" (plantlets) work?

Mature spider plants send out runners with baby plantlets that can be rooted in water or soil to grow new plants.

How much light does a spider plant need?

Bright, indirect light for the best variegation; it tolerates lower light but the colors fade.
