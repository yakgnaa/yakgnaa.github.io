Common Bird of Paradise problems

Root rot

Symptoms: Yellow leaves and a mushy stem base.
Likely cause: Overwatering.
What to do: Trim rotten roots, repot in a well-draining mix, and water less often.

Splitting leaves

Symptoms: Leaves develop natural splits along the veins.
Likely cause: This is a normal growth trait, especially outdoors in wind, not a disease.
What to do: No treatment needed — it's cosmetic and natural.

No flowers indoors

Symptoms: Healthy foliage but no blooms.
Likely cause: Insufficient light, and indoor plants often need several years of maturity to flower.
What to do: Provide the brightest light possible and be patient — flowering indoors is uncommon and slow.

FAQ

Is bird of paradise toxic to cats and dogs?

Yes, mildly — it can cause vomiting, nausea, or diarrhea if ingested.

Why aren't my bird of paradise leaves splitting like the ones outdoors?

Splitting is often wind-driven in outdoor plants; indoor plants in still air split less, and it's purely cosmetic either way.

Why won't my indoor bird of paradise flower?

It needs very bright light and typically several years of maturity — flowering indoors is uncommon.

How often should I water a bird of paradise?

About once a week, when the top 2 inches of soil have dried out.
