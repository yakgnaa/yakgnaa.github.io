Common ZZ Plant problems

Root or rhizome rot

Symptoms: Yellow, mushy stems and leaves, and a soft, black rhizome.
Likely cause: Overwatering — the most common ZZ plant killer.
What to do: Remove rotten parts, let the soil dry completely, repot in well-draining mix, and water far less often.

Yellowing stems

Symptoms: One or more stalks turn yellow all at once and feel soft.
Likely cause: Overwatering, or a stem stressed by physical damage.
What to do: Cut the yellow stem at the base, check for rot, and adjust your watering.

Slow or no new growth

Symptoms: The plant looks static for months.
Likely cause: ZZ plants grow slowly by nature, more so in low light or cool temperatures.
What to do: Move to brighter indirect light and be patient — new shoots often emerge in spring and summer.

FAQ

Is a ZZ plant toxic to cats and dogs?

Yes, it's toxic if chewed or eaten, and its sap can irritate skin — handle with care and keep it away from pets.

Why are my ZZ plant's stems turning yellow?

Usually overwatering; check the rhizome (the potato-like base) for softness or rot.

How often should I water a ZZ plant?

About every 2–3 weeks, letting the soil dry out fully between waterings.

Can ZZ plants survive low light?

Yes, they're very tolerant of low light, though growth slows further and it prefers bright indirect light.
