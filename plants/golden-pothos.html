Common Golden Pothos problems

Root rot

Symptoms: Yellow, mushy leaves and a soft, black stem base.
Likely cause: Overwatering, or standing water in a cachepot.
What to do: Trim rotten roots, repot in fresh well-draining soil, and water less often.

Mealybugs

Symptoms: Small cottony white clusters at leaf joints and on leaf undersides.
Likely cause: Introduced from another infested plant; thrives in dry indoor air.
What to do: Dab affected spots with a rubbing-alcohol-soaked cotton swab, then treat with insecticidal soap weekly until they're gone.

Leggy, sparse vines

Symptoms: Long bare stretches of vine with few leaves.
Likely cause: Not enough light.
What to do: Move closer to a bright window and pinch or prune vines to encourage fuller, bushier growth.

FAQ

Is pothos toxic to cats and dogs?

Yes, it contains calcium oxalate crystals that can irritate the mouth, tongue, and stomach if chewed.

Why does my pothos have yellow leaves?

Usually overwatering; occasional yellowing of a single old leaf is normal, but several yellow leaves at once points to soggy soil.

How do I make my pothos bushier?

Pinch back leggy vines just above a leaf node and give it brighter, indirect light.

Can pothos grow in water?

Yes, cuttings root easily in water and can live in it long-term with occasional water changes.
