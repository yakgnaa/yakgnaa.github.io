Common Heartleaf Philodendron problems

Root rot

Symptoms: Yellow, mushy leaves and soft, blackened stems.
Likely cause: Overwatering.
What to do: Trim rotten roots and stems, repot in fresh well-draining soil, and water less often.

Leggy vines with small leaves

Symptoms: Long bare stems and small, far-apart leaves.
Likely cause: Not enough light.
What to do: Move to brighter indirect light and pinch vines to encourage bushier growth.

Yellow leaves

Symptoms: Scattered yellowing, often starting with lower leaves.
Likely cause: Overwatering, or occasionally too little light or nutrients.
What to do: Check soil moisture before watering again, and feed lightly during the growing season.

FAQ

Is philodendron toxic to cats and dogs?

Yes, it contains calcium oxalate crystals that irritate the mouth and stomach if chewed.

Why is my philodendron leggy?

It's stretching toward light — move it closer to a bright window and prune to encourage fuller growth.

How often should I water a heartleaf philodendron?

About once a week, when the top inch of soil feels dry.

Can philodendron grow in water?

Yes, cuttings root easily and can be grown long-term in water with occasional water changes.
