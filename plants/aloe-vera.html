Common Aloe Vera problems

Root rot

Symptoms: Mushy, translucent, or brown leaves, and a soft base.
Likely cause: Overwatering or poor drainage — the most common aloe problem.
What to do: Remove from the pot, trim rotten roots, repot in cactus/succulent mix, and resume watering only once the soil is bone dry.

Thin, curling leaves

Symptoms: Leaves look thin, curled, or puckered.
Likely cause: Underwatering, or too little light causing weak growth.
What to do: Water thoroughly and move to a brighter spot.

Brown or rust-colored spots

Symptoms: Dry brown or rust spots on leaves.
Likely cause: Sunburn from sudden direct sun exposure, or fungal spot in humid conditions.
What to do: Acclimate gradually to brighter light, and improve air circulation if it's humidity-related.

FAQ

Is aloe vera toxic to cats and dogs?

Yes, ingesting the plant can cause vomiting and diarrhea, even though the gel is used topically in skincare.

Why is my aloe vera plant turning brown and mushy?

Almost always overwatering or a pot without drainage — aloe needs to dry out completely between waterings.

How often should I water aloe vera?

About every 2–3 weeks, less in winter — always let the soil dry out fully first.

Why are my aloe leaves thin and curling?

Usually underwatering or insufficient light; water deeply and move it somewhere brighter.
