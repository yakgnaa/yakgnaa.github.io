Common Phalaenopsis Orchid problems

Root rot

Symptoms: Brown, mushy roots and yellowing leaves.
Likely cause: Overwatering, or a non-orchid potting mix that holds too much water.
What to do: Trim rotten roots, repot in orchid bark mix, and water only when the roots turn silvery.

No reblooming

Symptoms: Healthy leaves but no new flower spikes.
Likely cause: Not enough light, or no temperature drop to trigger blooming.
What to do: Move to brighter indirect light; a natural nighttime temperature dip in fall can encourage a new spike.

Wrinkled leaves

Symptoms: Leaves look thin and wrinkled.
Likely cause: Root damage from rot, or underwatering.
What to do: Check the roots for rot; if they're healthy, water more consistently.

FAQ

Are orchids toxic to cats and dogs?

No, Phalaenopsis orchids are considered non-toxic, making them one of the safer flowering houseplants for pet owners.

How do I know when to water my orchid?

Check the roots through the clear pot — water when they turn silvery-green rather than on a fixed schedule.

Why won't my orchid rebloom?

It likely needs brighter indirect light, and sometimes a slight cool-down in fall to trigger a new flower spike.

Why are my orchid's leaves wrinkled?

Usually root damage from overwatering-related rot, or inconsistent watering — check the roots first.
