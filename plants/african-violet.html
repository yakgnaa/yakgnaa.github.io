Common African Violet problems

Crown or root rot

Symptoms: A mushy, collapsing center and soggy soil.
Likely cause: Overwatering, especially water sitting on the crown.
What to do: Water from the bottom or avoid wetting the crown, and repot in a well-draining African violet mix if rot has set in.

No flowers

Symptoms: Healthy leaves but few or no blooms.
Likely cause: Insufficient light, or an overly large pot delaying flowering.
What to do: Move to brighter indirect light; African violets often bloom best when slightly pot-bound.

Leaf spots from cold water

Symptoms: Pale or brown ring-shaped spots on leaves.
Likely cause: Cold water touching the leaves while watering.
What to do: Use room-temperature water and water the soil, not the foliage.

FAQ

Is African violet toxic to cats and dogs?

No, it's considered non-toxic and safe around pets.

Why does my African violet have spotted leaves?

Cold water splashed on the leaves is the classic cause — always use room-temperature water and avoid wetting the foliage.

Why won't my African violet bloom?

It likely needs brighter indirect light; being slightly root-bound in its pot can also encourage flowering.

How should I water an African violet?

Water the soil (not the leaves or crown) with room-temperature water, or water from the bottom via a saucer.
