Common Money Tree problems

Root rot

Symptoms: Yellow leaves, a soft trunk or braided stems, and mushy roots.
Likely cause: Overwatering — money trees are especially sensitive to soggy soil.
What to do: Trim rotten roots, repot in a fresh well-draining mix, and water less often.

Yellow leaves

Symptoms: Leaves yellow and drop, often several at once.
Likely cause: Overwatering is the most common cause; underwatering and low light are less common causes.
What to do: Let the soil dry out more between waterings and check the drainage.

Brown leaf edges

Symptoms: Crispy brown edges or tips.
Likely cause: Low humidity, or too much direct sun.
What to do: Increase humidity and move it out of harsh direct sunlight.

FAQ

Is a money tree toxic to cats and dogs?

No, it's considered non-toxic, making it a pet-safe houseplant choice.

Why is my money tree's braided trunk turning soft?

This usually signals root or trunk rot from overwatering — check the roots and repot if they're mushy.

Why are my money tree's leaves turning yellow?

Overwatering is the most common cause; let the soil dry out more between waterings.

How often should I water a money tree?

About every 1–2 weeks, when the top inch or two of soil has dried out.
