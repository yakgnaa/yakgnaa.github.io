Common Echeveria problems

Root rot

Symptoms: Mushy, translucent, or black leaves at the base.
Likely cause: Overwatering — the leading cause of Echeveria death.
What to do: Remove from the pot, trim rotten roots and leaves, repot in a fast-draining succulent mix, and resume watering only once bone dry.

Stretching (etiolation)

Symptoms: The rosette stretches upward, with leaves spreading apart and gaps forming.
Likely cause: Not enough light.
What to do: Move to a much brighter spot, ideally with some direct sun; existing stretched growth won't reverse, but new growth will be more compact.

Mealybugs

Symptoms: Small cottony white clusters between leaves.
Likely cause: Introduced from another plant.
What to do: Dab with an alcohol-soaked cotton swab, and treat with insecticidal soap if widespread.

FAQ

Is Echeveria toxic to cats and dogs?

No, Echeveria succulents are considered non-toxic and are a popular pet-safe choice.

Why is my Echeveria stretching out and losing its tight rosette shape?

It needs much brighter light — this stretching (etiolation) is permanent on existing growth, but new leaves will grow tighter with more light.

How often should I water Echeveria?

About every 2–3 weeks, letting the soil dry out completely between waterings.

Why are my Echeveria's lower leaves mushy?

Almost always overwatering — let the soil dry out fully and check for root rot.
