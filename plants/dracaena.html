Common Dracaena problems

Brown leaf tips or edges

Symptoms: Crispy brown tips, sometimes with a yellow border.
Likely cause: Fluoride in tap water, low humidity, or overwatering.
What to do: Use filtered or distilled water, raise humidity, and check the soil isn't staying soggy.

Root rot

Symptoms: Yellow, mushy lower leaves and a soft stem base.
Likely cause: Overwatering.
What to do: Trim rotten roots, repot in a well-draining mix, and water less.

Yellow, drooping leaves

Symptoms: Multiple leaves yellow and droop.
Likely cause: Overwatering, or occasionally too much direct sun.
What to do: Let the soil dry between waterings, and move it out of harsh direct light.

FAQ

Is Dracaena toxic to cats and dogs?

Yes, it can cause vomiting, drooling, and dilated pupils in cats if ingested.

Why does my Dracaena have brown leaf tips?

Usually fluoride or chlorine in tap water — try filtered or distilled water instead.

How often should I water a Dracaena?

About every 1–2 weeks, when the top of the soil has dried out; it's fairly drought-tolerant.

Can Dracaena tolerate low light?

Yes, most Dracaena varieties tolerate low to medium light well, though growth is slower.
