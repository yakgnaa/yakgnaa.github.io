Common Snake Plant problems

Root or stem rot

Symptoms: A mushy, discolored leaf base, and leaves collapsing or turning yellow/brown.
Likely cause: Overwatering — by far the most common cause of snake plant death.
What to do: Remove affected leaves, let the soil dry completely, repot in a fast-draining succulent mix, and water far less often.

Wrinkled or curling leaves

Symptoms: Leaves look thin, wrinkled, or curl inward.
Likely cause: Underwatering for an extended period, or root damage from prior overwatering.
What to do: Water thoroughly and check that the roots are still healthy; thirst-related wrinkling resolves within a week or two of normal watering.

Mealybugs or scale

Symptoms: Small cottony spots or brown bumps on leaves.
Likely cause: Introduced from another plant.
What to do: Wipe off with an alcohol-soaked cotton swab, and treat with insecticidal soap if it's widespread.

FAQ

Is a snake plant toxic to cats and dogs?

Yes, mildly — it can cause drooling, vomiting, or nausea if chewed, though serious poisoning is rare.

Why is my snake plant turning mushy at the base?

Almost always overwatering; snake plants need to dry out completely between waterings.

How often should I water a snake plant?

About once every 3–4 weeks — when in doubt, err on the side of underwatering.

Can a snake plant survive in low light?

Yes, it's one of the most low-light-tolerant houseplants, though it grows faster in bright indirect light.
